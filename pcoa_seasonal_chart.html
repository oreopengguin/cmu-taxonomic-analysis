The River Sorts Itself in Two

Each point is one of the top 20 OTUs, placed by principal coordinates analysis (PCoA) on the pairwise edit distance between full-length DNA sequences — computed with the dynamic-programming edit_distance code in the Alignment folder, no external aligner. Color marks the season each OTU was most abundant in; size marks its share of all top-20 reads. The first axis alone splits a winter/spring assemblage from a summer/fall one — sequence similarity and seasonal timing mostly agree, with one striking exception circled below.

Method: classical (Gower) PCoA — double-center the squared edit-distance matrix, eigendecompose, scale the top eigenvectors by √eigenvalue. Negative eigenvalues summed to <0.5% of the positive total, so the 2-D embedding is a faithful read of the distances. Reading it: proximity = sequence similarity, not physical or temporal proximity — color is an ecological overlay on a purely genetic map, so a tight cluster of different colors (see callout) means near-identical DNA recurring across seasons, not a mistake in the ordination.
